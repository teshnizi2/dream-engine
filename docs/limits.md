* **One fixed map, 64x64 pixels.** Training and evaluation use the same maze and the same textures. Nothing was tested on other levels, so nothing here says the model generalises to them, and it is not a general game engine.
* **It drifts.** Errors compound frame after frame: the dream tracks the real game (PSNR above 20 dB) for about the first @@TRACK20@@ frames and then goes to a different place. It stays *game-like* (see the validity table) but is no longer the same place as the real panel, so "PSNR against the real game" stops being meaningful after that point. "Wake up" re-seeds it from the real game, and the page marks every wake-up on the drift chart.
@@V3_LIMIT@@
* **It gets smoother over time.** Late in a long rollout the dreamed frames lose detail and settle on simple views (often a wall close-up): over the last 32 frames of the 512-frame rollouts they keep @@SHARP_PCT@@% of the real frames' edge sharpness. Edges are always softer than the real engine's pixel-exact ones, and thin features (window frames, brick joints, the rings on the pillar) are the first to blur.
* **Human play was not tested.** Training data is a sticky random walk with wall avoidance, not human play. Strafing (Q/E) is the rarest action, and backing up and strafing are among the lowest-scoring controls in the hold-one-key test; spinning in place or pressing into walls for a long time is further from the training distribution.
* **Small, single-run evidence.** One architecture trained at one scale; every comparison is a single run per setting, and differences below about 0.5 dB are within run-to-run noise (an extra 4k plain fine-tuning steps moved the rollout means by 0.4 dB), and the drift numbers depend on which episodes are drawn ("How stable are these numbers?"). Evaluation is on the game's own trajectories; there is no human study of how convincing it feels.
* **Speed is hardware-dependent.** The fps numbers are from one Apple M4 whose GPU was shared with other jobs, in headless Edge. Other GPUs, browsers and power states differ. Without WebGPU the page falls back to WASM: @@WASM_FPS@@.
* **Toy scale on purpose.** GameNGen fine-tunes a Stable Diffusion model and Genie is trained on internet video at far larger scale; this is a few million parameters, hours of A100 time, one map. It shows the recipe, not the state of the art.
* **Heun is not a verdict on Heun.** With two or three Heun steps and the sigma range 2e-3 to 5 the sampler diverged here (@@HEUN2_DB@@ and @@HEUN3_DB@@ dB one step ahead against @@EULER2_DB@@ for Euler), so Euler steps (as in DIAMOND) are used; more steps or a different schedule would likely work.
